Baiting

Baiting (or a bait-and-switch attack) is when scammers use false advertising to trick people into clicking a link to install malware on their device or giving their personal information to the website. If you want to avoid such scams, watch out for promises or deals that seem too good to be true (like my earlier promise to teach you guys how to make millions of dollars in a month). In this case, it was just a prank, but if I was an actual scammer, this would have been malware or a site that steals your info. You should also hover your cursor over links and see the URL in the bottom-left corner of the screen to check if they are real or fake.

Pretexting

Pretexting is when malicious actors construct fake scenarios that seem plausible to trick people into revealing personal information or clicking malicious links. The information gained from this can be used for follow-up attacks. To avoid this type of attack, ask the sender questions to verify their identity and do this verification in person or on a video call. If they can't answer the questions or provide proper identification, they may be a malicious actor.

Honeytrap Attacks

A honeytrap attack is when an attacker targets people looking for romantic connections online on dating websites or social media. The attacker will initially try to rizz the victim up and, after the victim is interested, try to get sensitive information from the victim or get them to go to a malicious website. Both of these options can be used to set the stage for a follow-up attack. To avoid this, watch out for any suspicious behavior or requests for personal information.

Tailgating/Piggybacking

Tailgating or piggybacking occurs when an unauthorized person follows an authorized individual through a secure door or access point. This can happen when an unauthorized person poses as a plausible individual (like a custodian or delivery person) to get an authorized person to let them in. This can also happen if an unauthorized person gains access to an authorized person's device and uses it to install malware or gain information about the person or the company.
